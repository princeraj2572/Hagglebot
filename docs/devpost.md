## Hagglebot

**Problem.** Getting a fair price means hours of research and awkward haggling with strangers. Most people just pay the sticker price, and sellers know it.

**Solution.** Hagglebot is an AI buyer agent. You enter what you want, your maximum price and how hard it should haggle (patient, balanced or quick). It opens chats with several sellers in parallel, anchors low, concedes in small steps, and closes only when a price fits your budget. Then it creates a PayPal order; you press "Confirm and Pay", approve in PayPal's checkout, and Hagglebot sends the seller a confirmation containing the PayPal payer-id as proof. A paid deal can be fully refunded from the dashboard through the PayPal Payments API, so the whole order lifecycle (create, capture, refund) is covered. A live dashboard shows every thread, the latest offers, and a Pause killswitch.

**Agentic commerce, done honestly.** Hagglebot always introduces itself as an AI buyer agent and never impersonates you. The code, not the prompt, decides every price, so the budget can't be exceeded. The language model only phrases each move, and its output is checked: wrong prices, budget leaks and claims to be human are rejected. It walks away after repeated refusals, at the message limit, or on scam signals such as hidden fees and off-platform payment. In our demo, "Shady Sam" tries exactly that and is refused. Every one of these decisions shows up as a **guardrail note** under the message it shaped, so you can see why the agent did what it did, not just that it did it.

**Why it matters.** Haggling is a tax on people who dislike it, and on anyone without the time. An agent that negotiates for you only works if you can trust it with two things: your budget and your money. So the product is not "an AI that talks to sellers", it is the trust layer around it: a hard budget ceiling the model cannot override, a disclosure that it is an AI, scam walk-aways, a full explanation of every decision, and payment through a checkout you approve yourself. As agents start to buy on our behalf, that is the part every one of them will need, and here it is built, tested and visible.

**Where it goes next.** The seller side is a one-method `Seller` interface (`respond(context)`), and today it is backed by simulated personas so the demo is repeatable and safe. A real marketplace would be one more adapter behind that same interface: Hagglebot would read a listing, talk to the seller through the marketplace's own messaging, and the rest (strategy, guardrails, PayPal checkout, refund) stays unchanged. The same engine could also help sellers answer lowball offers consistently. We have built the listings half (Channel3 search); contacting real sellers and a live marketplace adapter are not built.

**Real listings.** "Find real listings" searches Channel3's product catalog for the item and turns the real retailer offers (store, price, condition) into sellers. Each one is played by a simulated agent that opens at the real price; no retailer is contacted and only friendly personalities are used for real stores. The result is real prices to negotiate against, with the negotiation itself still simulated.

**Tech.** Next.js 15, TypeScript, Tailwind, SQLite, PayPal Orders v2 (official server SDK plus the JS checkout), Channel3 product search, and Gemini Flash-Lite with an automatic Groq fallback and a template negotiator if no model is reachable.

**Honest limits.** This is a demo: PayPal sandbox only (no real money), and the sellers (four in the demo, fourteen to choose from) are simulated AI personas, not real listings. Real listings come from Channel3 search only: no retailer is contacted and nothing is bought from one. The scam detector is heuristic.

**Try it.** MIT licensed; clone, add sandbox credentials, run `pnpm dev`, and press "Load demo scenario".
